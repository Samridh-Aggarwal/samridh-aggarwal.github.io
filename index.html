Samridh Aggarwal

I study human behaviour with machines and machine behaviour with humans.

Whether the learner is a person in a lab or a network on a GPU matters less than you would expect. The same questions apply to both: what it takes from feedback, what it ignores, and how small differences in the machinery turn into large differences in behaviour. I started out asking them about people. Now I mostly ask them about models.

I try to answer any one question with every method that can speak to it, because the methods fail in completely different ways. A proof can be sound and say nothing. A simulation can be internally perfect and describe nothing real. An experiment can be beautifully run and measure the wrong thing. A trained network can be right for reasons nobody can recover. A claim that survives all four is unlikely to be surviving by accident. The work is mostly in the instruments, not the results, and most of what I have learned so far came from catching faults in my own setup.

prior-pull

When you hand a language model somebody's demographics and ask how they answered a survey, does it read the person, or just react to being handed a profile? Twelve models scored against gibberish placebos. Instruction tuning installs the reaction, by factors of 6 to 83 across every family tested, but only two of five instruct models respond to a real person more than to nonsense, and one responds measurably less.

perception-gap

Do frozen vision encoders trained on photographs tell meaningful satellite changes from cosmetic ones? Twelve encoders on EuroSAT tiles, perturbed in ways that alter content and ways that do not. All land above chance and none near ceiling, more pretraining data measurably helps for a fixed objective, and a satellite-native model carries an orientation prior no photo model has.
